\section{The Velocity Vector}
\emph{Velocity} is the rate of change of position with respect to time. Since position is relative, so is velocity. 

There are two types of velocity. Average velocity and instantaneous vecocity. You calculate average velocity as you would any average rate of change, here as the change in position divided by the change in time.

Calculating instantaneous velocity usually requires ideas of differential calculus. But in the special case of uniform circular motion, there is another way.

The key ideas are these. At any moment, the instantaneous velocity points in the direction of motion and has length equal to the speed.

For a beetle moving around a circle at a constant speed the velocity is always perpendicular to the position vector of the beetle relative to the center of its path.

\item As an ant rotates about the point $A(3,-2)$ at a constant rate of $1/5$ rad/sec as it crawls clockwise around a circle centered at the origin. Find the velocity of the ant as it passes the point $P(7,-10)$.  All coordinates are measured in cm.

\end{enumerate}

\begin{explanation}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Since the velocity vector is tangent to the circular path and in the direction of motion, we can find its direction by rotating the position vector (coordinates in cm)
\begin{align*}
  \overrightarrow{AP} &= \overrightarrow{\answer{OP}} - \overrightarrow{\answer{OA}} \\
                                &= \langle \answer{-8}, \answer{4} \rangle - \langle \answer{3} , \answer{1} \rangle  \\
                                &= \langle  \answer{-11} , \answer{3} \rangle  
\end{align*}
$\pi/2$ radians counterclockwise. This gives the vector
\[
 {\bf w} = \langle \answer{-3} , \answer{-11} \rangle \text{ cm}.
\]
Since the velocity vector ${\bf v}$ points in the same direction as ${\bf w}$ and has length $v = 2$ cm/sec equal to the speed, we just need to scale ${\bf w}$ by the appropriate factor to get ${\bf v}$. This factor is
\begin{align*}
    \lambda  &= \frac{v}{|{\bf w}|}  \\
                  &= \frac{\answer{12} \frac{\text{cm}}{\text{sec}}}{\answer{\sqrt{130}\text{ cm}}} \\
                 &= \answer{\frac{12}{\sqrt{130}}} \text{ sec}^{-1} .
\end{align*}

So the beetle's velocity as it passes the point $P$ is
\begin{align*}
   {\bf v}  &= {\bf w} \left( \frac{v}{|{\bf w}|} \right)  \\
               &= \left \langle  \answer{\frac{-36}{\sqrt{130}}} ,  \answer{\frac{-132}{\sqrt{130}}}\right \rangle \frac{\text{cm}}{\text{sec}}.
\end{align*}

\item This one is easier since the ant's speed is equal to the product of its rotation rate about the center of its circular path and the radius of that path.

As the ant passes the point $P$, the vector
\[
 {\bf w} = \langle  \answer{-8} , {-4} \rangle \text{ cm}
\]
points in the direction of its motion and has length equal to the radius of the path. So the ant has velocity
\begin{align*}
   {\bf v} &= \left( \frac{1}{5} \frac{\text{ rad}}{\text{sec}}\right)  \langle  \answer{-8} , {-4} \rangle \text{ cm}  \\
              &= \langle \answer{-1.6} , \answer{-0.8} \rangle \text{ cm/sec} 
\end{align*}
as it passes $P$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{explanation}
\end{question}
